The money is close. The behaviour is not.

Cost is within ~1% at these churn rates — so pick on incentive design, not savings. What changes is what you are buying: the trickle pays for partial success, the milestone pays only for the outcome you actually want.

And it matters here specifically because the architect's influence is front-loaded. They diagnose, architect and hand off — client success owns the relationship afterwards. So you are not buying ongoing attention, you are buying quality of decision at the point of sale: don't sell bad-fit clients, don't spec builds that fail. Retention is simply how that decision gets verified nine months later.

The risk to design around

Milestones create cliffs. An architect watching a client wobble at month 11 has real incentive to do something desperate — discount, over-promise, hide a problem — to get them over the line. And the moment the 12-month bonus lands, their interest drops to zero.

Two milestones instead of one is the mitigation: an earlier win at 6 months, so it is never a single distant cliff. If you want, a third at 24 months keeps them caring past the first year.
